As of publishing this July 20th 2026, we are closing in on the 2-year anniversary of the publication of the Draghi report. Not to regurgitate the entire report, but in short it outlined;

an investment shortfall of EUR 750 - 800 Bn. 

fragmentation in the single market.

an urgent need for sovereign capability building

I’d like to focus on the first.

It is true that Europe structurally underinvests in our infrastructure, in technology, and in ourselves. 

The most widely discussed investment shortfall is venture capital, where Europe has historically been investing ~5x less that it’s US peers (see picture)

What is Venture Capital?

Venture capital, in short, is an asset class developed to underwrite the additional risk that comes with if a new technology is possible to build, if it can be built in such a way that it can be sold with a profit, and if anyone wants to buy/use it. 

Since, there is no way for an investor to know the answers to any of this prior, they will use some proxies to argue for a certain investment becoming a success. They’ll argue that the team has a unique right to succeed based on their experience, character traits, they may have a chip on their shoulder driving them to try harder than anyone else, or another of a thousand different reasons. They call this pattern matching. A more accurate term would be Dark Magic.

As the probability of any of these investment not going to zero is extremely low. We make the argument that; if a venture capital investor makes enough investments, with similar, but slightly different arguments. One of them are bound to succeed. That one investment will have to return enough, through a sale, to pay for everything that did not work. This is what is called the Power Law.

What have been the response to the investment deficit?

Since 1984, with the introduction of the First Framework Programme, Europe have had a practice of creating supranational investment vehicles, to invest in basic research.

With the introduction of Horizon 2020 in the Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF), the instruments changed to finance innovation and merged research and commercial financing under one umbrella. Which have been expanded with the most recent Horizon Europe under the MFF from 2020 to 2028.

The investment deficit highlighted by Draghi was heard by the politicians in the EU. Since then;

The Horizon Europe budget have been proposed t increase from EUR 95,5 Bn to EUR 175Bn

The European Scale Up fund of EUR 5Bn have been established and anchored by the EU

The European Competitiveness Fund, a proposed EUR 200Bn fund, is currently being voted on in the parliament

A total of EUR 380Bn, and still short EUR 370Bn of the lower bound of the investment deficit. 

The Eurocrats heard that investment were missing, and the response were for the politicians to invest the tax payers money into speculative technology.

Henry Ford supposedly once said “If I had asked people what they wanted, they would have said faster horses.” The problem was stated as there’s not enough investment in Europe, and the policians listening interpreted this as they should invest more. This is the lazy answer, it is them giving people the faster horse.

And considering that the total annual budget of the EU is EUR 160Bn to EUR 180Bn, it fair to say that the EU’s balancesheet cannot carry this investment increase itself.

What is Draghi’s Car?

If we examine what Draghi said, then we find that Europe have an investment shortfall of EUR 750 to 800 Bn. compared to geopolitical equals such as the United States and China. That is equivalent to a 5 percentage point increase in the investment-to-GDP ratio. In comparison, the Marshall plan after World War II was about 1 percentage point.

Draghi himself, stated that this cannot be achieved by public financing alone. It included some suggestions for how we might unlock this level of investment;

implementing a Capital Markets Union, or as it is called today Savings and Investment Union

harmonising regulation across the union, which EU-Inc has made a valient attempt at achieving

incentivising people to invest some of their savings, where ~30% of European wealth sits in liquid cash and bank deposits compared to the US with ~15%

reforming banking and prudential rules to review and revise some of the heavy banking regulation implemented after the 2008 financial crisis

This provides some interesting insights into symptoms and treatment options, but what is the underlying problem?

If Europe need private investors to invest more into their companies, infrastructure, and debt, we need to examine the incentives for the investors to do just this.

We have focused on venture capital in this piece, and we will continue to do so. 

For any private investor to invest in anything, it requires a belief that their investmentt will be returned with a profit. Venture capital usually requires the investors to be invested for 10+2 years, meaning a standard 10 year horizon with a provision allowing the fund to extend by 2 years, if they have an investment which is performing very well.

The prospect of a Limited Partner (LP), meaning the individual or institution investing into a venture capital fund, investing into a fund and not seeing returns for +10 years, means they will have to discount the erosion of value from inflation and the opportunity cost of investing in something else.

That means the fund should deliver on average ~15% year over year.

Today, only the top decile of European funds deliver returns that match this. Pairing this with an LP base that do not have experience in investing in venture capital, meaning they concetrate their investments into few funds, and we have an asset class that is perceived to lose.
