The AI as an existential threat discussion have been gaining traction the last few days after the former Anthropic researcher, Jacob Coxon, posted his reason for resigning on X. Jacob Coxon on x.com

He was arguing that the frontier model labs are racing to what the industry calls RSI (Recursive Super Intelligence), and since the media have been running with the narrative of the evil AI companies who continue to develop a technology that they believe will kill us all.

This doomsday prophecy is (unfortunately) not new in the AI industry:

When OpenAI released ChatGPT-2, in 2019, they chose to delay due to the risk of mass misuse and disinformation

When the Reasoning models were released, Ilya Sutskever (co-founder of OpenAI) warned that the ‘Thinking Machines’ were too dangerous to release

When Anthropic released their Mythos model, they had to pre-release to a select few critical industries, due to extensive cybersecurity risks from it’s hacking capabilities

Etc.

I would argue the case that it is a very clever marketing strategy, and an attempt at regulatory capture.

The Deep Moated Duopoly

Today, frontier AI development have already been consolidated in 2 (maybe 3) companies. Here, frontier refers to the most capable models. Currently, this includes OpenAI and Anthropic who consistently release the most capable models, and potentially Elon Musk’s XAI, though I haven’t seen evidence of the Grok models competing with GPT-6-Astra or Claude Mythos.

Previously, we would have considered Google Deepmind as frontier, though the Gemini model releases have not proven to have significant leaps in capability, and Google is generally pursuing other value streams with the AI resources. And Meta have not been counted as a real competitor for years.

We’ve effectlively created a duopoly in what is widely believed to be a larger revolution than the internet.







OpenAI’s Sep 9 proposal asks Congress for mandatory capability testing, independent assessments, cyber controls, incident reporting. Ask who can most easily pay that bill.
