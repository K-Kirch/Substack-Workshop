Now, since none of the venture capital investors have enough cash to invest themselves, they will raise it from other funds and individuals. Who, in turn, will require a return, since there is no gurantee that any of the investments will work. Usually, this is a return of 2x or more, and a good venture capitalist will argue they can deliver a 3x return. This means, the one good investment before that pays for the failures, now has to return enough that the initial fund will double at minimum.

As a fund, I will receive payments based on the 2&20 structure, meaning I take a 2% management fee to run the fund and pay a salary to myself and collegues. If the fund is a success, meaning I return more than the promised 3x, I receive a 20% carry of anything above.

This is no easy task, and if we perform a little math, you’ll see why;

Total fund of EUR 20Mn

As the fund manager, I argue that I have a 5% probability of any one of my investments succeeding. Which means, I’ll invest up to EUR 1Mn in each company.

I invest EUR 1Mn for 15% of a company.

Let’s say this is a company developing a new, biodegradable battery. This EUR 1Mn, does not allow to finish development, begin their sales, and build enough revenue to pay their bills.

The  company will raise new capital a few times, and every time we issue new shares of the company. Meaning, the founders and I, as an investor, will be diluted equally.

The company is a success, it is going through a sale to a larger company, and my ownership of the company have changed from 15% to 5%

Now, remember, my EUR 20Mn total fund need to return EUR 40Mn to satisfy my own investors, EUR 60Mn for me to do well, and only if I return more than EUR 60Mn do I share in the returns as a fund.

For this one investment to return enough that my fund reaps a benefit, we can calculate the required  value of my 5% stake.

EUR 60Mn = x * 0,05

EUR 60Mn / 0,05 = EUR 1,2Bn







If we assume a standard inflation of 2% per annum and an average return of an index fund of 8% per annum, then a venture capital investment have to meaningfully beat the 8%.

The performance of a venture capital fund is best measured by Distribute Paid-In Capital (DPI), and a good fund should deliver a 3x or more on their original fund size. This an 11,61% average annual return over 10 year.

Currently, the top decile European venture capital fund delivers a DPI of ~1,8 to 2,5 within 10 years where US delivers ~2,5 to 3,2. But US venture capital funds deliver on average deliver DPI faster than European, meaning within a 10-15 year horizon both deliver a DPI of 3,0 to 4,5.

Why is this? There are two primary causes;

highly active M&A market

deep and liquid public markets for IPOs
